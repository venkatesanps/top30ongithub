Merchant hierarchy, from processing entity to transaction division

Every card transaction posts at one place: the Transaction Division, the leaf of the tree, whose number is the merchant ID. Every level above it exists to group that activity for contracts, fees, statements and reports. Funding follows its own line, the funds transfer instruction (FTI), which doesn't have to match any branch of the tree.

Three things that sit beside the tree

These group TDs differently from the parent-child line. Mixing them up is the most common cause of totals that don't reconcile.

Funds Transfer Instruction (FTI)

The instruction that moves money to a bank account. JPMorgan's reporting docs state that funding occurs at the FTI level. One FTI can collect several TDs, and TDs under one Company can fund to different accounts.

A Financial Activity Summary run at Company level combines all FTI activity and may not match any single bank statement. Run it per FTI to reconcile to the bank.

Reporting group (RG)

A named set of entities chosen for reporting, from anywhere in the tree. Reports accept RG as an entity type next to PE, CO, BU, TD and FTI.

An API request for a reporting group can carry only one entity type and ID. In Resource Online the equivalent was a custom hierarchy of up to three levels, such as 10 of a corporate office's 85 locations.

Tax ID

The merchant's taxpayer ID cuts across the tree. Visa assesses its monthly Fixed Acquirer Network Fee (FANF) across all accounts under one taxpayer ID. Form 1099-K is issued in the merchant's IRS filing name.

Group TDs by tax ID, not by Company, when checking FANF or tax reporting. confirm where the tax ID is stored in the merchant set-up.

Above the merchant: who sits over the tree

The PE-to-TD tree is the merchant's own structure. Above it sit the parties that move the money and carry the risk. Money and liability travel down different paths: a processor touches every transaction but holds no funds, and an ISO sells the account but usually carries no losses.

Filled dot: money passes through this party, or it carries liability for the merchant's transactions. Hollow: it doesn't. ISO and PayFac arrangements vary by contract.

The same tree under three business models

Who owns the TD changes the keys you join on. In a direct account the merchant's own TDs are the business key. Under a payment facilitator, many sub-merchants share the facilitator's TDs, so the sub-merchant ID becomes the key.

Older hierarchies you will still meet

Chase's front ends and older portals each used their own level names. The leaf is always the merchant location; the names above it change. Leaves are drawn at the bottom so equivalent levels line up.

Dashed boxes are reserved for Chase Paymentech. Resource Online's guide says most merchants on the FDMS-style back end use only the bottom three levels, which roughly match NPCO 1, Company and TD. Front-end reports link back to the merchant through the Reporting Merchant # and, on Tandem (PNS), the PNS Merchant #.

Gateway hierarchies use other names

Gateways keep their own account trees. Visa Acceptance Solutions (Cybersource) uses portfolio, merchant, structural and transacting organizations. These are the gateway's IDs, not Chase TD numbers, so map them through the processor set-up rather than by name.

How the hierarchy shows up in merchant reports

Choosing the level

  • Current reports take an entity picker: PE, CO, BU, TD, FTI or RG. Report file names and headers carry the Processing Entity ID.
  • In Resource Online, users start at the highest level they're entitled to. They move with Drill Down and Drill Up, the Selected Hierarchy list, Quick Search by hierarchy or merchant number, and Top Level.
  • Pre-generated reports are built at the user account's hierarchy level and can't be switched to another level.
  • Statements are chosen by hierarchy or merchant number.

Fields that carry the level

  • Entity Type Code: "the level at which the reported event occurred" (PE, CO, BU or TD).
  • Entity Identifier: the CO, BU, TD or PE number that goes with it.
  • Transaction Division External Identifier: part of the key of the Deposit Details report.
  • Resource Online fields: Reporting Merch #, Client #, Division, Chain ID. Chargeback cases carry CO, BU and TD.

Traps

  • Add a Company's total and its Business Units' totals together and you count the same money twice. Sum only one level.
  • Rows posted above the TD, such as account fees, have no TD. Joining every row to a TD drops them.
  • A Company-level funding summary won't match one bank statement. Reconcile per FTI.
  • Changing a custom hierarchy can make month-to-month comparisons unreliable. Keep history of the tree.

Modelling it in the warehouse

Keep the tree as its own versioned dimension. Post facts at the level in Entity Type Code, then roll them up through the path that was valid on the event date, so a TD that moved to another Business Unit doesn't rewrite history. FTI and reporting-group membership go in bridge tables because they don't follow the tree.

-- One row per node at any level, versioned so a TD can move
create table dim_merchant_hierarchy (
  entity_type   char(2),      -- PE, CO, BU, TD
  entity_id     varchar,      -- the number at that level
  parent_type   char(2),
  parent_id     varchar,
  pe_id varchar, co_id varchar, bu_id varchar, td_id varchar,  -- flattened path
  platform      varchar,      -- Salem (6-digit MID) or PNS (12-digit)
  valid_from    date,
  valid_to      date          -- '9999-12-31' for the current row
);

-- Funding and reporting groups don't follow the tree
create table bridge_fti_td   (fti_id varchar, td_id varchar, valid_from date, valid_to date);
create table bridge_rg_entity (rg_id varchar, entity_type char(2), entity_id varchar);

-- Roll every fact up to Company as the tree stood on the event date
select h.co_id, f.event_date, sum(f.amount) as amount
from fact_financial_event f
join dim_merchant_hierarchy h
  on  h.entity_type = f.entity_type_code
  and h.entity_id   = f.entity_identifier
  and f.event_date between h.valid_from and h.valid_to
group by h.co_id, f.event_date;
